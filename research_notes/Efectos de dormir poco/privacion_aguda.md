# Acute TOTAL sleep deprivation in humans (24 h, 36 h, 48 h, 72 h+ and recovery)

Scope: human studies of total sleep deprivation (TSD). Where only partial-restriction data exist (e.g., one night of ~4 h), this is flagged explicitly. No animal data used. Verification status: items marked "[press-figure]" come from press releases or press quotes rather than the paper's abstract; items marked "[not verified this session]" are well-known findings whose primary text I could not open.

## What measurable changes occur at 24, 36, 48, 72 h without sleep?

### Takeaway
Impairment starts well before 24 h. At about 17-19 h awake, performance is roughly equal to 0.05% BAC, and beyond about 19-24 h it is close to 0.10% BAC. By 24-36 h there are large attention lapses, about 60% greater amygdala reactivity, more pain sensitivity, and higher ghrelin and hunger. By 48 h, perceptual distortions and hallucinations are reliably reported. By 72 h+ there are multimodal hallucinations and delusions, with a psychosis-like state around day 5.

### Cited Findings

**Alcohol equivalence**
- Dawson & Reid (1997), *Nature* 388:235. 40 subjects in a counterbalanced design: 28 h awake vs alcohol to a mean BAC of 0.10%, measured on hand-eye tracking. Performance fell ~0.74% per hour awake between hours 10 and 26, and ~1.16% per 0.01% BAC. Moderate fatigue caused more impairment than the legal alcohol limit. In the paper, ~17 h awake ≈ 0.05% BAC and ~24 h ≈ 0.10% BAC (equivalences commonly derived from these slopes). DOI 10.1038/40775 — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/40775); [IDEAS/RePEc summary](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v388y1997i6639d10.1038_40775.html)
- Williamson & Feyer (2000), *Occup Environ Med* 57(10):649-655. Within-subject design, n=39 (30 transport workers, 9 army personnel), 28 h TSD vs alcohol up to ~0.1% BAC. At 17-19 h awake (about 22:30-01:00), performance on some tests was equal to or worse than at 0.05% BAC. Response speeds were up to 50% slower on some tests and accuracy was worse. Beyond ~19 h, performance matched the 0.1% BAC level. The abstract gives no per-test numbers and no specific 24 h value — [PMC1739867](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1739867/)

**Attention / PVT / microsleeps**
- Lim & Dinges (2010), *Psychol Bull* 136(3):375-389, DOI 10.1037/a0018883. Meta-analysis of 70 articles and 147 cognitive tests, all TSD <48 h. Largest effect: lapses in simple attention (g = −0.776, 95% CI −0.96 to −0.60). Smallest: reasoning accuracy (g = −0.125, NS). Domains ranked between these: complex attention, working memory, processing speed, short-term memory. Hours awake was the only moderator that predicted between-study variance, and only for accuracy measures — [PMC3290659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3290659); [UPenn PDF](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/LimDinges2010MetaAnalysis.pdf)
- Doran, Van Dongen & Dinges (2001): 10-minute PVT every 2 h across 88 h of TSD. Performance variability grew with time awake, which supports "wake-state instability" (the brain switching between wake and sleep within seconds, i.e., microsleeps/lapses) — [search summary; WSU record](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Sustained-attention-performance-during-sleep-deprivation/99900546553901842)
- Dinges, NTSB presentation: performance becomes unstable after one night without sleep. After 3 nights, ~14% of subjects had lapses lasting up to 30 s. Example: ~42 lapses per 20-min test sleep-deprived vs ~4 rested (the slides do not give the exact deprivation duration) — [NTSB slides](https://ntsb.gov/news/events/Documents/1a_Dinges.pdf)
- Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges (2003), *Sleep* 26(2):117-126. 14 days at 4, 6 or 8 h in bed, compared with 3 nights of TSD (0 h). TSD produced disproportionately large neurobehavioral and EEG delta-power responses for the amount of sleep lost. Cumulative wake beyond ~15.84 h predicted lapses in all conditions. Subjects were largely unaware of their growing deficits. The common takeaway that 14 nights at 6 h ≈ 1 night of TSD and 4 h ≈ 2 nights of TSD comes from this paper's figures [not verified this session; abstract retrieved did not state it] — [PubMed 12683469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/); [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040087752)

**Emotional reactivity**
- Yoo, Gujar, Hu, Jolesz & Walker (2007), *Curr Biol* 17(23):R877-R878, "The human emotional brain without sleep — a prefrontal amygdala disconnect." n=26 (ages 18-30), ~35 h awake vs sleep control, fMRI with negative images. The amygdala was hyperreactive and its connectivity with the medial prefrontal cortex was reduced, with stronger coupling to the autonomic brainstem. Walker quote: emotional centers "over 60 percent more reactive" [press-figure] — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/10/071022124729.htm); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/86512/)

**Pain**
- Krause, Prather, Wager, Lindquist & Walker (2019), *J Neurosci* 39(12):2291-2300. n≈24 healthy young adults, one night of TSD. Pain thresholds dropped (more temperatures were rated painful). Pain reactivity rose in primary somatosensory cortex and fell in the striatum and insula. Press figure: S1 activity +126% [press-figure]. An online sample (>230 adults) found night-to-night sleep changes tracked next-day pain — [PMC6433768](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6433768); [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2019/01/28/sleep-pain-connection)

**Hormones / metabolism**
- Schmid et al. (2008), *J Sleep Res* 17:331-334. n=9 normal-weight men, crossover of 7 h vs 4.5 h vs TSD. After TSD, ghrelin was ~22% higher than after 7 h, and hunger was 3.9 vs 1.7 (7 h) and 2.2 (4.5 h). Morning leptin was unchanged — [PubMed 18564298](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18564298/)
- Insulin sensitivity. Donga et al. (2010), *JCEM* 95(6):2963 [PARTIAL deprivation: one night of ~4 h vs normal sleep, n=9]. Hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp: endogenous glucose production 4.4 vs 3.6 µmol/kg LBM/min (hepatic insulin resistance). Glucose disposal 32.5 vs 40.7 (≈ −20%, peripheral resistance). FFA 68 vs 57 µmol/L. I found no clamp study after one night of total deprivation — [JCEM](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/95/6/2963/2598601); [PubMed 20371664](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20371664/)
- Testosterone. Su et al. (2021) meta-analysis (18 studies, 252 men): TSD SMD −0.64 (p<0.001), partial deprivation SMD −0.22 (NS). Figures come from secondary summaries because I could not open the primary paper — [CGU record](https://pure.lib.cgu.edu.tw/en/publications/effects-of-sleep-deprivation-on-serum-testosterone-concentrations/)
- Growth hormone and cortisol, 40 h wake study (n=10 young men). The nocturnal GH surge disappeared during deprivation and was intensified during recovery sleep. Mean 24 h cortisol was not changed. The nocturnal cortisol rise came ~1 h earlier during deprivation and ~1 h later with recovery sleep. Other small studies conflict on cortisol (some report it lower or unchanged after 24 h) — [search summary incl. PubMed 10898125](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10898125/); [Endocr Regul](https://www.sav.sk/journals/endo/endo202.htm); [PMC1188300](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1188300/)
- Dinges et al. (1994): cortisol and ACTH did not change across 64 h of TSD — [PMC294300](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC294300)

**Immune**
- Dinges et al. (1994), *J Clin Invest* 93(5):1930-1939. n=20, 64 h of TSD. NK subpopulation counts (CD16, CD56, CD57) fell after one night, and CD56/CD57 rose after two nights. At maximum deprivation, WBC, granulocytes, monocytes and NK cytotoxicity were all higher. Recovery sleep removed the increases — [PMC294300](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC294300)
- Irwin et al. (1996), *FASEB J* [PARTIAL: awake 22:00-03:00, n=42 men]. NK activity fell and returned to baseline after one recovery night, but IL-2 production stayed suppressed — [PubMed 8621064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8621064/)
- IL-6/TNF: these are the cytokines most often reported to rise after acute sleep loss (monocyte TLR-4-stimulated IL-6/TNF up after partial deprivation, Irwin 2006/2008). Results vary with protocol and circadian timing, and several studies are null — [UCLA Irwin 2008 PDF](https://teams.semel.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Sept%202008%20-%20Sleep%20loss%20activates%20cellular%20inflammatory.pdf)
- Frey, Fleshner & Wright (2007), *Brain Behav Immun*, 40 h of TSD. Secondary sources disagree on what it found: some say IL-1β rose, one says IL-6 fell, and others cite IL-6 and IL-1β increases — [CU record](https://vivo.colorado.edu/display/pubid_21684); [Frontiers 2022 citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.945661/full)

**Cardiovascular**
- Kato et al. (2000), *Hypertension* 35:1173-1175. n=8, one night without sleep. Resting MAP rose 82→86 mmHg (p=0.012). MSNA fell 28→22 bursts/min (p=0.017). HR, forearm vascular resistance and catecholamines did not change significantly. The authors concluded the pressor effect is not caused by sympathetic vasoconstriction or tachycardia — [experts.umn.edu](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/effects-of-sleep-deprivation-on-neural-circulatory-control/)
- Later 24 h TSD work found sex differences, with MSNA rising only in women in one study. A review concludes the BP and autonomic response to acute TSD is still unclear — [PMC3403737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3403737)

**Hallucinations / perceptual distortion**
- Waters, Chiu, Atkinson & Blom (2018), *Front Psychiatry* 9:303, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00303. Systematic review of 21 studies, n=760, deprivation from 24 h to 11 nights. 20 of 21 studies (95%) reported perceptual distortions or hallucinations: visual 90%, somatosensory 52%, auditory 33%. Mood changes 76%, disordered thought 66%, depersonalization 52%, delusions 42% (40% in the Discussion), time distortion 20%. Time course:
  - <24 h: anxiety, irritability
  - 24-48 h: distortions, depersonalization, temporal disorientation; simple hallucinations ~30-48 h; reliably seen by 48 h (87.5% cumulative)
  - ~50 h: complex multimodal hallucinations
  - ~72 h: delusions
  - ~day 5: turning point to a state like acute psychosis or toxic delirium
  - [PMC6048360](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6048360/)

### Inferences
- The two alcohol-equivalence studies agree closely. Normal daytime wakefulness that runs to about 1 a.m. puts many people at a performance level matching legally impaired driving in most countries (0.05%).
- Simple attention (lapses/PVT) is the most sensitive and best-quantified marker. Effects on higher reasoning are small at <48 h, so a sleep-deprived person may "feel fine" on complex thinking while missing stimuli.
- Hormone and immune changes after a single night are modest and often small-n (n=8-20). Cognitive and emotional effects are larger and more robust.

### Gaps
- I did not find exact PVT lapse counts at 24 vs 36 vs 48 vs 72 h from a single primary source; the Doran 2001 and Van Dongen 2003 full texts were not accessible.
- I could not verify the exact 17 h ≈ 0.05% and 24 h ≈ 0.10% conversion figures from the Dawson & Reid full text (paywalled). The slopes are verified.
- The 60% (amygdala) and 126% (S1 pain) figures come from press materials.
- I found no clamp-measured insulin sensitivity after one night of TOTAL deprivation (only 4 h partial). IL-6/TNF findings after TSD are inconsistent, and the Frey 2007 direction is unresolved.
- Data beyond 72 h are mostly historical, uncontrolled studies (Waters 2018 caveat).

## How large are the effects (percentages, effect sizes)?

### Takeaway
Simple attention lapses show a medium-to-large effect (g ≈ −0.78) at <48 h. Performance after 17-24 h matches 0.05-0.10% BAC, and response speed is up to 50% slower. The reported physiological changes are about +22% ghrelin, +4 mmHg MAP, ~−20% glucose disposal (partial deprivation), and testosterone SMD −0.64.

### Cited Findings
- Lim & Dinges 2010 effect sizes:
  - Simple-attention lapses: g = −0.776
  - Reasoning accuracy: g = −0.125 (NS)
  - [PMC3290659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3290659)
- Performance decline: −0.74% per hour awake (hours 10-26) vs −1.16% per 0.01% BAC — [Nature 1997](https://www.nature.com/articles/40775)
- Speed up to 50% slower at 17-19 h — [Williamson & Feyer 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1739867/)
- Ghrelin +22%, hunger more than doubled (3.9 vs 1.7) — [Schmid 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18564298/)
- MAP +4 mmHg (82→86), MSNA −21% (28→22 bursts/min) — [Kato 2000](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/effects-of-sleep-deprivation-on-neural-circulatory-control/)
- Glucose disposal −20% (40.7→32.5) after one 4 h night (partial deprivation) — [Donga 2010](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/95/6/2963/2598601)
- Testosterone SMD −0.64 for TSD (secondary report) — [Su 2021 record](https://pure.lib.cgu.edu.tw/en/publications/effects-of-sleep-deprivation-on-serum-testosterone-concentrations/)
- Amygdala +60% [press-figure] — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/10/071022124729.htm); S1 pain activity +126% [press-figure] — [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2019/01/28/sleep-pain-connection)
- Hallucinations or distortions in 95% of the 21 TSD studies — [Waters 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6048360/)

### Inferences
- Effect sizes for attention are among the largest in behavioral sleep research. Endocrine effects of a single night are real but smaller and more variable between people.

### Gaps
- No pooled effect sizes found for immune markers or BP after acute TSD.
- No verified percentage for the testosterone drop after exactly 24 h (a "~25%" claim on a fitness site had no primary source and was excluded).

## What does one recovery night restore and what doesn't it?

### Takeaway
One recovery night largely normalizes immune cell changes and subjective alertness after ~24-64 h. It only partly restores working memory after 36 h and does not restore all immune function (IL-2). After very long deprivation (~100 h), recovery takes multiple nights (~50% of hours lost), and a minority have lingering symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- After 36 h of TSD, an 8 h recovery night attenuated but did not fully reverse working-memory deficits (n=42 men; "effects of RS appear to be limited") — [Frontiers Neurosci 2023, PMC10151529](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10151529/)
- A 36 h TSD protocol with 4 recovery nights (R1-R4) and PVT every 2 h (n=83) was designed for multi-night recovery; that paper focuses on PVT validity — [Frontiers Neurosci 2022, PMC8885985](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8885985/)
- After 64 h, recovery sleep eliminated leukocytosis and higher NK activity, in parallel with recovery of neurobehavioral function — [Dinges 1994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC294300)
- After partial deprivation, NK activity returned to baseline after one recovery night but IL-2 stayed suppressed — [Irwin 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8621064/)
- GH secretion rebounds (is intensified) in recovery sleep after 40 h awake, and the cortisol rhythm shifts ~1 h later — [PubMed 10898125](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10898125/)
- After long deprivation, recovery took roughly 50% of the time awake (e.g., ~50 h of sleep after ~100 h). 7 studies (n=169) reported full recovery. 5 studies reported some participants with residual confusion, low mood, mania or delusional ideas for days to weeks. Follow-up ranged from 1 day to 6 months — [Waters 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6048360/)
- After 27 h of TSD, post-recovery PVT was not affected by caffeine dose, which suggests recovery with sufficient sleep — [PSU record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/caffeine-effects-on-recovery-sleep-following-27-h-total-sleep-dep/)
- Subjects in chronic and total deprivation protocols underestimated their own impairment, so self-rated "I feel recovered" is unreliable — [Van Dongen 2003, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040087752)

### Inferences
- Plausibly, one night largely resets the effects of ~24 h of TSD, but for ≥36 h some cognitive deficits (working memory) carry over to the next day. Recovery cost grows with deprivation length, but non-linearly: it is not hour-for-hour (~50%).

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source with exact PVT lapse recovery after one night following 24/36/40 h of TSD.
- No data found on when hormones (ghrelin, testosterone, insulin sensitivity) return to normal after one recovery night following TSD.
